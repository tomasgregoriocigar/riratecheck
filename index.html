== WINTER BILL CREDITS (YOU KEEP THESE IF YOU SWITCH) ==

Two state credits land on Rhode Island electric bills this year. Both are fixed-dollar line items on the delivery side of your Rhode Island Energy bill — they have nothing to do with who supplies your electricity:

  • Hold Harmless credit — $14.50/month, on bills rendered October 2026 through September 2027 (a full 12 months, not just winter). Every active electric distribution account gets it. Tariff R.I.P.U.C. No. 2304 (PUC Docket 25-45-GE).
  • RGGI emergency credit — $20.45/month in each of the January, February and March 2027 billing periods, for distribution accounts in rate classes A-16 (standard residential) and A-60 (low-income residential) — which is effectively every Rhode Island household. Tariff R.I.P.U.C. No. 2309 (PUC Docket 26-28-EL).

In January–March 2027 the two stack: $34.95/month. Most comparison sites ignore these credits entirely. We show them as a separate line in the math above — they lower every option equally, so they never inflate a supplier’s “saving,” and the supply-only savings figures stay clean.

CONFIRMED: Switching suppliers does not cost you these credits — they’re on the delivery side of your bill, and every Rhode Island household gets them. Third-party supplier customers and municipal aggregation customers keep both credits on the same terms as default-service customers, because the credits follow your Rhode Island Energy distribution account — not your supplier.

== FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS ==

What is the Last Resort rate?

The Last Resort rate (Last Resort Service) is Rhode Island Energy’s default supply rate — what you pay for the supply part of your bill if you have not chosen a third-party supplier or been auto-enrolled in a municipal aggregation program. For October 1, 2026 through March 31, 2027 it is 17.029¢/kWh, approved by the RI Public Utilities Commission (tariff R.I.P.U.C. No. 2096). It resets every April 1 and October 1; the summer 2026 rate was 11.092¢/kWh. It is the rate to beat — and in winter, beating it honestly is harder than the ads suggest.

Is switching worth it?

Sometimes — but often not. An analysis of RI Division of Public Utilities data found that between 2017 and 2024, third-party supply customers paid $61.6 million more in total than they would have on default service, and nearly every large supplier cost its customers money on average. Switching only makes sense when all of these hold: the rate clearly beats the Last Resort rate after any fees, the term spans the expensive winter months instead of expiring into them, the early-termination fee is low or $0, and the supplier’s Rhode Island track record is clean. If your town has municipal aggregation, you are probably already close to the lowest-cost option in the offers we list. Staying put is a valid answer.

What is municipal aggregation?

Municipal aggregation (community electricity) is when a city or town buys electricity supply in bulk for its residents. Eligible households are auto-enrolled, and anyone can leave or change tiers at any time with no fee. In Rhode Island, eight municipalities run programs with consultant Good Energy and supplier NextEra Energy Services: Barrington, Central Falls, Narragansett, Newport, North Kingstown, Portsmouth, Providence, and South Kingstown. For winter 2026–27 their Standard rate — the community default — is 16.929¢/kWh, and the Basic tier is 16.705¢/kWh. Use the town checker above to see your town’s full pricing. North Providence does not have a program.

Do contracts auto-renew in RI?

No. Rhode Island law prohibits residential electricity supplier contracts from auto-renewing. When your term ends, the contract ends — you choose a new offer or return to the default Last Resort service. Two cautions: a supplier can still offer you a new contract at a new rate when your term ends, so read end-of-term mail carefully; and a low introductory rate that expires mid-winter can leave you shopping at the worst moment. This site flags terms and renewal behavior whenever the source publishes them.

What’s the max early-termination fee?

By Rhode Island law, an early-termination fee on a residential electricity supply contract is capped at $50 — or twice your average monthly bill, if that is less (R.I. Gen. Laws § 39-26.7-6(a); Division rule 815-RICR-30-05-2 § 2.6). Many plans charge $0. This site shows each plan’s fee when it is confirmed (for example, Direct Energy’s Live Brighter 12 is confirmed at $0 on the supplier’s own site) and marks it “not confirmed” when the source does not publish it — we never guess.

Do I lose the state winter credits if I switch suppliers?

No. Both state credits are fixed-dollar line items on the delivery side of your Rhode Island Energy bill, and neither one is tied to who supplies your electricity. The Hold Harmless credit is $14.50/month on bills rendered October 2026–September 2027 (Tariff R.I.P.U.C. No. 2304). The RGGI emergency credit is $20.45/month in each of the January, February and March 2027 billing periods, for distribution accounts in rate classes A-16 and A-60 — the standard and low-income residential classes (Tariff R.I.P.U.C. No. 2309). Because the credits follow your distribution account, third-party supplier customers and municipal aggregation customers keep both, on the same terms as everyone else.
