> IF A SUPPLIER PAYS A REFERRAL FEE

  • It will be disclosed on that offer’s card. If a supplier may pay us a referral fee when a visitor enrolls, that offer’s card will say so in plain text on the card itself, next to the link — for example: “REFERRAL FEE: this supplier may pay us if you enroll through a link on this site.” You will not have to visit this page, open a footnote, or click anything to see it.
  • Payment never affects ranking. Offers are ranked by estimated supply cost at the stated usage, with track-record-flagged suppliers grouped separately, exactly as described in our Methodology. A supplier cannot pay to move up, and a supplier that pays nothing is ranked by the same math.
  • Payment never affects flags or the Top Honest Pick. Track-record flags come only from the sourced data described in the Methodology. The Top Honest Pick badge is awarded only by its five stated criteria. No fee changes a flag, removes a warning, or earns a badge.
  • “Stay put” stays on the table. If the default Last Resort rate or your town’s municipal aggregation rate is the lowest-cost option for your usage in the offers we list, we will say so — including when a paying supplier’s offer is worse.
  • Paid placement, if it ever exists, will be labeled as advertising. Any future featured placement paid for by a supplier will be marked “ADVERTISING / PAID PLACEMENT” on its face and will sit outside the honest ranking.

> WHAT A REFERRAL FEE IS

A referral fee (sometimes called an affiliate commission or an enrollment bounty) is an amount a supplier pays a website when a visitor who arrived through that website’s link enrolls with the supplier. The visitor does not pay the fee, and the supplier’s rate to the visitor is set by the supplier’s own offer and contract summary — but the existence of the fee is a financial connection between this site and that supplier, which is why it is disclosed on the card where the link appears, in line with the FTC’s Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255).
